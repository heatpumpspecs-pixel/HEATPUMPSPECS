# SEAI domestic heat-pump requirements and evidence sources

**Review date:** 2026-11-11  
**Purpose:** Source-discovery and requirements note for HEATPUMPSPECS. This is research documentation, not a product record and not a substitute for the cited primary documents.

## 1. Current governing SEAI document

The current SEAI *Domestic Technical Standards and Specifications* located during this review is **Version 4.0, July 2026**. It supersedes the Version 3.0, October 2025 PDF previously encountered in search results. Use Version 4.0 as the current reference unless SEAI publishes a later revision.

Primary source: https://www.seai.ie/sites/default/files/publications/Domestic-Technical-Standards-and-Specifications.pdf

Relevant provisions:

- **Clause 6.8.1, pages 76–79:** SEAI home-energy-upgrade grant eligibility criteria for heat-pump systems.
- **Clause 6.8.4, pages 85–86:** heat-pump handover documentation.
- **Clause 6.8.5, pages 86–88:** regulatory framework.
- **Clause 6.8.6, pages 89 onward:** heat-pump standards and guidance documents.

## 2. What the current domestic SEAI standard requires

Clause 6.8.1 lists the following grant eligibility requirements:

1. Compliance with the applicable Energy Labelling and Ecodesign regulations, including Commission Regulation (EU) No 811/2013, Commission Regulation (EU) No 2023/2048 for air-to-air heat pumps, and Ecodesign Commission Regulation (EU) No 2016/2282.
2. CE marking.
3. Design, installation, commissioning and maintenance in accordance with the relevant standards, including NSAI S.R. 50-4.
4. Electrical installation considerations under I.S. 10101.
5. An air-permeability test result where applicable.
6. An Ecodesign technical datasheet based on:
   - I.S. EN 14825 for space heating;
   - I.S. EN 16147 for water heating;
   - I.S. EN 15316-4-2 for heat-pump energy requirements and efficiencies.

SEAI also requires the heat-pump system to meet the minimum DEAP Seasonal Performance Factor (SPF) requirements in DTSS Table 8. The system is to be assessed in DEAP at design stage and before installation.

**Certification distinction:** the DTSS v4.0 eligibility list expressly identifies CE marking and regulatory/test-data requirements. A search of the document text for “KEYMARK” returned no match. Therefore this document does not establish Heat Pump KEYMARK as a separate mandatory product-certification requirement for the domestic SEAI grant. This is a statement about the cited DTSS document, not a claim that no other programme-specific condition can apply.

The DTSS also contains installer/contractor competence requirements. These include a certificate of competence from the specific heat-pump manufacturer and registration on the SEAI Renewable Energy Installers Register for heat pumps. Installer competence certification must not be confused with product certification.

## 3. Product identity and handover evidence

Clause 6.8.4 requires handover documentation to identify the installed equipment. It calls for the heat-pump brand name and nameplate details, including manufacturer, product identifier (model/part number) and a unique serial number.

The Ecodesign technical datasheet is to declare the test data based on the standards above. The document also requires other installation and commissioning evidence, including heat-load calculations, commissioning certificate, electrical completion certificate, F-Gas company certificate where applicable, collector documentation where applicable, manufacturer warranty and operating/maintenance manuals.

For HEATPUMPSPECS, this reinforces the need to keep model, model number/qualifier, system type, test data, source document and regional applicability distinct. A certificate or datasheet must be tied to the exact model/variant it covers.

## 4. HARP and DEAP are data sources, not interchangeable certification labels

SEAI describes HARP (Home-heating Appliance Register of Performance) as a domestic heating-appliance efficiency database used by BER assessors in DEAP. SEAI states that product submissions include product test data, product identification documentation and HARP submission forms.

Primary source: https://www.seai.ie/plan-your-energy-journey/for-your-business/energy-efficient-products/harp-database

The current SEAI HARP page provides a heat-pump submission pack and a link to search the database. The page does not, by itself, establish that every heat pump eligible for an SEAI domestic grant must have a HARP listing.

The current DEAP Guide version 4.3.1 page for non-default heat-pump data states that Ecodesign / EN 14825 / EN 16147 data may be used where applicable; HARP data can be imported, but heat-pump data not listed in HARP can still be entered manually where the required test data and evidence are available.

Primary source: https://deapguide.seai.ie/space-heating/heating-systems/heat-pumps/non-default-data

## 5. Heat Pump KEYMARK: what it establishes, and what it does not

The Heat Pump KEYMARK scheme describes itself as a voluntary, independent European certification mark (ISO type 5), based on independent third-party testing. The scheme is owned by CEN and CENELEC; certificates are granted by independent certification bodies.

Primary source: https://keymark.eu/en/products/heatpumps

The KEYMARK recognition page states that in Ireland the Heat Pump KEYMARK is an acceptable source of Ecodesign data for heat pumps and is used in the publication of energy performance certificates and building-regulation compliance checks. It also states that the Irish HARP database was in the process of being updated to include KEYMARK-certified products.

Primary source: https://keymark.eu/en/products/heatpumps/where-is-heat-pump-keymark-recognized

**Qualification:** the recognition statement is published by the KEYMARK scheme itself. Its statement about the HARP update is not independently confirmed by the current SEAI HARP page reviewed here. Do not treat the KEYMARK recognition page as an SEAI rule making KEYMARK certification compulsory for domestic grant eligibility.

A certificate should be checked individually for certificate number, holder, product type, exact sub-type/model/qualifier, testing basis, issue date, expiry date and current validity. A certificate covering one model must not be assumed to cover an entire product family.

## 8. Collection implications

For product-level records, the evidence path should be:

1. Identify the exact manufacturer, product family, model and qualifier.
2. Locate the official manufacturer Ecodesign technical datasheet and installation/product documentation.
3. Check the exact model against HARP where relevant to DEAP data; absence from HARP is not by itself proof that no valid test data exists.
4. Locate the exact Heat Pump KEYMARK certificate where one is claimed or available; verify model coverage and certificate validity.
5. Retain test standards, climate conditions, temperature application, capacity, COP/SCOP, sound data and qualifiers exactly as stated by the source.
6. Create one TechSpecs V2 product record for each distinct marketed model/variant where specifications differ. Keep the source sections and normalized values separate as required by the V2 standard.

No product specifications have been inferred or created by this research note.

## 9. Separate SEAI Triple E product register

SEAI's general energy-efficient-products page explicitly distinguishes the Triple E Register from HARP: Triple E is a searchable register of energy-efficient products; HARP is the product-efficiency database for home-heating appliances.

Primary sources:
- https://www.seai.ie/plan-your-energy-journey/for-your-business/energy-efficient-products
- https://www.seai.ie/plan-your-energy-journey/for-your-business/energy-efficient-products/triple-e-register-for-products
- Filtered heat-pump listing: https://triplee.seai.ie/acaproducts/ProductDetails.aspx?search=1&tech=52

The filtered Triple E listing contains Grant Aerona HPR290i160 as Triple E code HPU117632, product code HPR290i160, valid from 09/04/2025, with displayed CoP 4.72. The listing row does not state the test point or conditions for that CoP. SEAI states that Triple E products meet minimum energy-efficiency criteria and that businesses selecting listed equipment may use the Accelerated Capital Allowance (ACA) tax incentive. This is separate from HARP and must not be represented as domestic SEAI heat-pump grant approval.

## 10. Heat Pump KEYMARK searchable database — Grant Engineering (Ireland) ULC

The Heat Pump KEYMARK certified-products page embeds the searchable database at https://www.heatpumpkeymark.com/?type=109126. At the time of review, the database displayed **13,086 models and 3,244 subtypes** across certificate holders. The Grant Engineering (Ireland) ULC holder page listed nine subtypes, each with one model:

- Aerona R290: HPR290i40, HPR290i65, HPR290i90, HPR290i120 and HPR290i160.
- Aerona3 R32: HPID6R32, HPID10R32, HPID13R32 and HPID17R32.

The holder page and individual subtype pages link directly to certificate PDFs and offer per-subtype model CSV export. The holder page also offers a ZIP download of reports for all subtypes. The live database is searchable; the repository now retains a URL-based source inventory at `data/grant/heat-pump/source-inventory.json`, following the V2 source-inventory pattern. It records the nine subtype-page URLs and nine certificate-PDF URLs without duplicating the PDFs.

For the five Aerona R290 subtypes, the linked BRE certificates are dated 14 January 2025 and state validity through 13 January 2035. For the four Aerona3 R32 subtypes, the one-page certificates show issue date 1 March 2022 but do not display an expiry date in the retrieved text; current validity must be rechecked in the live database before being asserted.

This is a snapshot of the Grant holder's KEYMARK listings, not a complete Grant product catalogue, not proof that every model is currently sold in Ireland, and not proof of domestic SEAI grant approval.
